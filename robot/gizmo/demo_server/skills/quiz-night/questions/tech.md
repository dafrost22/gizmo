# Round: Computers and the internet

Ten questions. For each: the answer, what else to accept, and a line to say afterwards.

1. How many bits are in a byte?
   - Answer: eight.
   - After: Enough to count from 0 to 255.

2. Who is often called the first computer programmer?
   - Answer: Ada Lovelace.
   - Accept: Ada, Lady Lovelace, Countess of Lovelace.
   - After: In 1843 she published a program for Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine, a machine that was never finished.

3. Who invented the World Wide Web?
   - Answer: Tim Berners-Lee.
   - After: He proposed it in 1989 while working at CERN, the physics lab near Geneva.

4. What is two to the power of ten?
   - Answer: 1,024.
   - After: Which is why a kilobyte was long counted as 1,024 bytes.

5. What does the "HT" in HTTP and HTML stand for?
   - Answer: hypertext.
   - After: Hypertext is text with links in it, which is the whole idea of the web.

6. Which programming language is named after a British comedy group?
   - Answer: Python.
   - After: Its creator, Guido van Rossum, was a fan of Monty Python's Flying Circus.

7. In what year did the first iPhone go on sale?
   - Answer: 2007.
   - After: It was announced in January 2007 and went on sale that June.

8. What does MCP stand for, in the world of AI assistants?
   - Answer: Model Context Protocol.
   - After: It's an open standard for connecting an AI model to tools and data, and it's how I'm reading these questions.

9. Which mathematician proposed a test for whether a machine can pass for a human in conversation?
   - Answer: Alan Turing.
   - After: He described it in 1950 and called it the imitation game.

10. What was the first message sent over the ARPANET, the network that grew into the internet?
    - Answer: "LO".
    - Accept: L-O, "lo", or "login" if they explain that it crashed partway.
    - After: It was meant to be "login", in 1969, but the system crashed after two letters.
